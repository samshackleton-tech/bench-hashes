Where the proof experiment stands (step 2, continuing). Nothing of it is in 0.9 or asks anything of it; it is on my fork, and what of it enters the library is John's to decide, one piece at a time.

**The first piece offered, for after 0.9:** `Hash::to_hex` as two kernels proved as assembled, one for each architecture, with source, compiler and proof in the tree, as John asked on #370. A draft: [`candidate/sam-hex-proved`](https://github.com/samshackleton-tech/BLAKE3/tree/candidate/sam-hex-proved), folder `tools/verify/hex` (its README says what is claimed and what is not). It gets its own issue when 0.9 is out.

**What the experiment is now** ([`experiment/sam-machine-code-lean`](https://github.com/samshackleton-tech/BLAKE3/tree/experiment/sam-machine-code-lean/tools/verify/machine), `tools/verify/machine`; `lang/DESIGN.md` is the intent, `./prove_all.sh` checks everything from the source in one command):

- **Code is written in a small language of its own**, kernels and word-level functions in one file. Its compiler prints the assembly and writes the proof; Lean checks the proof of the instructions as assembled. The compiler is not trusted: a wrong print or a wrong proof is a theorem Lean refuses.
- **Every function has a contract a reader reads, in Lean, and the theorem is "the assembled code meets it".** Proved so far: `hex` (with what an observer of time and cache sees), `push`, `merge` (and a second `merge` on another kernel), `add` (push, then merge), a loop that calls `merge`.
- **A function calls another by its contract and by nothing else.** `add` is proved from the theorems of `push` and `merge`, not their code; putting the second `merge` behind the contract changes 4 of the 147 lines of `add`'s generated proof.
- **A contract names no machine.** It is written once, of bytes at addresses (`spec/`); each machine model says once what meeting it means. `hex` and `push`, each from one source, meet one contract on x86-64 (a model written here) and on AArch64 (Google DeepMind's model, unchanged). `merge` and `add` so far on x86-64.
- **Each proof is audited**: an unchanged copy must be accepted, then changed instructions and false statements must each be refused. `merge`: 14 changed instructions, 4 of its checked entry, 12 false statements and 5 changed source clauses, all 35 refused. `hex`: 10 instructions and 11 false statements on x86-64, 7 changes on AArch64. `push` on AArch64: each of its 18 instructions made to do nothing, and 5 false statements. The contracts that name no machine: 15 false statements. With each instruction made a no-op in turn, `push` (26), `add` (32) and the loop (38) refuse all but two: moves in `add` of a value already in place, which the audit found.
- **What a reader reads to know what a theorem says** is asked of Lean, not kept by hand (`INDEX.md` there lists each theorem with it): 508 lines for `push` to 931 for `add` on x86-64, of which the machine model is 343; about 6,200 on AArch64, of which Google's model is 6,113. What is written for one function is a few of those: `push`'s contract is eight lines.
- **The function a user calls, `hash`, has its contract and no theorem yet** (`spec/HashContract.lean`: the 32 bytes at `out` become the specification's BLAKE3 of the message). What there is, is a test: the source, run by the language's meaning with each kernel as its contract says, meets it on the test vectors' 35 lengths and three platforms (104 examples), and four wrong sources are each refuted.

What it does not do yet: prove `hash` (the proved functions are the tree's stack and the hexadecimal: 306 of the 1,258 x86-64 instructions the language's code compiles to); loops and calls on AArch64; any statement about an instruction's own time; Lean reading x86-64 bytes itself (a script reads the object's listing); and the proofs lean on `bv_decide`'s axioms beside Lean's three.

Checked from nothing on rented machines at e9574d86 (every function, both architectures, every audit: one run, 45 minutes on 64 threads); the runs are published with the evidence (`process/fast-feedback/language-and-contracts` on the bench-hashes fork's `process/sam-fast-feedback`).
